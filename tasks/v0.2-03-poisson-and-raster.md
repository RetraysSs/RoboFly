## Objective

Generate Poisson spike trains to stimulate neurons at a chosen rate, and use them to drive a random excitatory/inhibitory network. Plot the activity as a spike raster.

## Background

**Poisson spike trains.** Stimulating a neuron "at 100 Hz" is commonly done with a Poisson process: in every time step the neuron receives a spike with probability `p = rate * dt / 1000` (rate in Hz, `dt` in ms), independently of the other steps. The average rate is `rate`, but the individual spikes are irregular. The same approach is used to stimulate neurons in published connectome-based models (check the methods section of Shiu et al. 2024 for details). In this project the Poisson spikes of the sensory neurons will encode robot sensor values.

**Fano factor.** For a Poisson process the variance of the number of spikes in a fixed window equals its mean (Fano factor = 1). The tests use this as a check.

**Spike raster.** A raster plot shows one dot per spike: time on the x axis, neuron index on the y axis. It is the standard way to inspect network activity.

## Interface

File: `src/flybot/inputs.py`

```python
def poisson_spikes(rate_hz, n_steps, dt, n=1, rng=None):
    """Return a boolean array of shape (n_steps, n).

    rate_hz: scalar, or array of shape (n,) with one rate per neuron
    dt:      time step in ms
    rng:     numpy.random.Generator (for reproducibility); default: a new one
    Raises ValueError if rate_hz * dt / 1000 is outside [0, 1].
    """
```

The result is multiplied by a weight and passed to `LIFNetwork.run(..., jump_ext=...)`.

## Experiment

File: `experiments/v0_2_random_network.py`

1. Build a random network with N = 200 neurons, 10% connectivity (no self-connections), about 20% of the neurons inhibitory. Use a `scipy.sparse` matrix.
2. Drive every neuron with Poisson spike trains and simulate 500 ms.
3. Plot the raster and the population firing rate over time. Print the mean firing rate and the number of silent neurons.
4. Save the figure to `docs/figures/v0_2_random_network_raster.png`. Seed all random number generators and derive the number of steps from the duration.

Starting point that gave activity with mean rates of about 10 Hz and no silent neurons in a test run (`t_ref = 2 ms`): excitatory weight +1.0 mV, inhibitory weight -3.0 mV (a column of the weight matrix belongs to one presynaptic neuron, so all outgoing weights of a neuron have the same sign), Poisson drive of 100 Hz with a weight of 6 mV. Vary the drive and the weights and describe what changes: a network that is silent, a network in which every neuron fires at a high rate, and the range in between.
